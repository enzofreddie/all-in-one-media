## Final voiceover script
Did you know the Library of Alexandria probably didn't die in one great fire? Its rulers were so hungry for books that ships in the harbor were searched, their scrolls copied, and the owners often got only the copies back. In 48 BC, a fire in Julius Caesar's war may have burned scrolls near the docks. But the library likely lived on, and died slowly: lost funding, scholars driven out, and centuries of neglect. It didn't go up in flames. It simply faded away. Follow for more.

## Fact notes (why the wording is hedged)
- Ship searches: Britannica says ships were searched and books either returned or confiscated and replaced with a copy "with adequate compensation"; it calls these "fabulous stories" -> "often got only the copies back" is the reported practice.
- 48 BC fire: ancient sources (Plutarch, Cassius Dio, Seneca) disagree on what burned; Cassius Dio says warehouses of grain and books near the docks. Britannica's summary still says the main library was destroyed in 48 BC, so the script says "may have burned" and "likely lived on" (not "survived").
- Slow decline: Ptolemy VIII expelled the scholars c. 145 BC; Roman-era funding/patronage fell; the Bruchion quarter was devastated under Aurelian (270s CE); the daughter library at the Serapeum was destroyed in 391 CE. Most modern historians describe a gradual decline rather than one fire.
